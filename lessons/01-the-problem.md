# Lesson 1: The problem: notes that get lost

Time: 5 minutes. Theme: framing.

## Learning objective

Explain why investigation notes kept outside the tool are lost at handover, and what a Timeline offers instead.

## Tell: the problem

- The value of a hunt or investigation is the record: what was asked, what was found, what was ruled out.
- Today, notes live in chat threads, personal documents and ticket comments. At shift change, or six weeks later, the next analyst cannot answer:
  - What did we already check on this host?
  - Was this behaviour accepted, and by whom?
  - Which indicators did we find?
  - What goes in this week's client report?

## Tell: what a Timeline is

- A **Timeline** is a saved investigation workspace in Elastic Security.
- It holds three things together: the query (the question), the events that matter, and the notes that explain them.
- Because it is a saved object in the tool, it is shared, searchable and still there next month.

## Tell: roadmap

1. Investigate inside a Timeline and keep notes there (lessons 2 to 4).
2. Name and structure Timelines so the team can search them (lessons 5 and 6).
3. Let a Workflow collate the notes into a weekly report (lessons 7 and 8).

## Show: what to look for

- The Timelines list is already a shared, searchable history.
- Each row is a record that could have been a chat thread.

## Tell: the recap

- Notes outside the tool are lost at handover. A Timeline keeps the query, events and notes together.
- One idea to keep: the record is the product.
- Next: how a hunt is run in a Timeline.
